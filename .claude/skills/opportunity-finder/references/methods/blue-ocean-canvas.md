# Blue Ocean Strategy Canvas

**Lens:** Market · **Method 17 of 21** · **Output:** `opportunity-output/17-blue-ocean-canvas.md`

**Question:** What could the business eliminate, reduce, raise or create to stand apart instead of competing head-on?

## Why it matters

Matching competitors factor by factor leads to price wars. A different value curve can open uncontested space.

## Use when

- Mature or crowded markets; differentiation questions.

## Needs

- Factors the industry competes on
- How competitors and we score on each (with evidence)
- Business profile (`opportunity-output/business-profile.md`)

## Steps

1. List 6–10 factors the industry competes on. Score the industry average and 1–2 key competitors 0–10.
2. Apply the four actions to each factor: eliminate, reduce, raise, create. Every factor gets an action or "keep".
3. Draw the current and new curves with `scripts/value_curve.py`.
4. Check the new curve has focus, divergence and a tagline a customer would repeat.
5. Identify which non-customers the new curve could attract.

## Checks

- [ ] Every factor has an action.
- [ ] Scores labeled.
- [ ] New curve visibly diverges.
- [ ] Material claims labeled per `references/evidence-rules.md`

## Output

- Strategy canvas (SVG)
- Eliminate / reduce / raise / create grid
- New curve and tagline
- Non-customers it could attract
- **Signals for the map:** 1–3 candidate opportunities this supports or weakens, each with strength (strong / moderate / weak / assumed) and source IDs

## Reading the signal

A divergent curve tied to customer evidence is a moderate-to-strong market signal.

## Script

`scripts/value_curve.py`. Paste its output; do not retype numbers.
