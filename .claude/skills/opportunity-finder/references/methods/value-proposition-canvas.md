# Value Proposition Canvas

**Lens:** Offer · **Method 05 of 21** · **Output:** `opportunity-output/05-value-proposition-canvas.md`

**Question:** Does the offer relieve the pains and create the gains customers rank highest?

## Why it matters

Many offers are aimed at what is easy to build, not what customers care about most. The canvas makes the fit, and the gaps, visible.

## Use when

- A customer profile exists (from JTBD or ODI) and an offer is proposed.

## Needs

- Customer jobs, pains and gains with evidence
- Current or proposed offer
- Business profile (`opportunity-output/business-profile.md`)

## Steps

1. Customer profile: jobs, pains (risks, frustrations, obstacles) and gains (outcomes, benefits), ranked by importance to the customer.
2. Value map: for each pain and gain, the specific pain reliever or gain creator. If none exists for a top item, write "none".
3. Fit: which top pains and gains are addressed, which are not, and whether the strongest relievers target what customers rank highest.
4. Name the gap to close first.

## Checks

- [ ] Ranking by customer importance, not company convenience.
- [ ] Every reliever traced to a pain or gain.
- [ ] Unaddressed top items named.
- [ ] Material claims labeled per `references/evidence-rules.md`

## Output

- Customer profile (ranked)
- Value map
- Fit assessment
- Gap to close first
- **Signals for the map:** 1–3 candidate opportunities this supports or weakens, each with strength (strong / moderate / weak / assumed) and source IDs

## Reading the signal

Strong fit on top-ranked pains with evidence is a strong signal; fit only on low-ranked items weakens the candidate.
