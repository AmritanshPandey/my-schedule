# Five Forces Map

**Lens:** Market · **Method 15 of 21** · **Output:** `opportunity-output/15-five-forces-map.md`

**Question:** Is the space structurally attractive, and which force limits profit?

## Why it matters

A growing market can still be a bad business if buyers, suppliers or substitutes take the profit.

## Use when

- Entering or expanding in a market.

## Needs

- Market definition
- Competitor, buyer, supplier and substitute evidence
- Business profile (`opportunity-output/business-profile.md`)

## Steps

1. Rate rivalry, threat of new entrants, threat of substitutes, buyer power and supplier power: high / medium / low, each with specific evidence.
2. State overall attractiveness for this business, not the industry average.
3. Name the limiting force and a counter-position (for example, lock-in through workflow integration against buyer power).

## Checks

- [ ] Each rating has evidence.
- [ ] Substitutes include non-consumption and AI alternatives.
- [ ] Counter-position is specific.
- [ ] Material claims labeled per `references/evidence-rules.md`

## Output

- Forces table
- Overall attractiveness
- Limiting force and counter-position
- **Signals for the map:** 1–3 candidate opportunities this supports or weakens, each with strength (strong / moderate / weak / assumed) and source IDs

## Reading the signal

Low limiting forces support the candidate; a high force with no counter-position is a blocking signal.
