# Productization Matrix

**Lens:** Offer · **Method 08 of 21** · **Output:** `opportunity-output/08-productization-matrix.md`

**Question:** Which parts of expert or service work are repeatable enough to become products?

## Why it matters

Service businesses often hold product-shaped components hidden inside custom work. Packaging them scales revenue beyond headcount.

## Use when

- Service, consulting or expert-led businesses.

## Needs

- Service broken into steps
- How much varies by client
- Capacity to build and maintain a product
- Business profile (`opportunity-output/business-profile.md`)

## Steps

1. List components: diagnosis, assessment, delivery steps, deliverables, follow-up.
2. Rate each for repeatability (same across clients) and standardizability (fixed process vs judgment): high / medium / low.
3. For high-high components, pick a format: assessment, workshop, template, guided workflow, software or AI-assisted tool. Say why that format fits.
4. Keep low-repeatability or judgment-heavy components custom, explicitly.
5. Note where AI could make a medium component productizable, as `[HYPOTHESIS]` with the test.

## Checks

- [ ] Each component rated on both axes.
- [ ] Format justified by the component.
- [ ] Judgment-heavy parts kept custom.
- [ ] Material claims labeled per `references/evidence-rules.md`

## Output

- Component matrix
- Productized formats
- What stays custom
- First product to build
- **Signals for the map:** 1–3 candidate opportunities this supports or weakens, each with strength (strong / moderate / weak / assumed) and source IDs

## Reading the signal

High-high components that customers already buy repeatedly are strong capability-to-offer signals.
