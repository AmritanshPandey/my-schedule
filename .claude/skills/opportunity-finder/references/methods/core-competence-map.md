# Core Competence Map

**Lens:** Capabilities & Assets · **Method 11 of 21** · **Output:** `opportunity-output/11-core-competence-map.md`

**Question:** What is the business distinctively good at, and where else could it apply?

## Why it matters

A core competence opens many markets; a skill that is merely good opens none.

## Use when

- Looking for adjacencies or diversification.

## Needs

- Candidate capabilities with evidence of performance
- Business profile (`opportunity-output/business-profile.md`)

## Steps

1. Test each candidate on three questions: does it give access to many markets, does it make a real difference to customer benefit, is it hard to imitate?
2. Classify as core competence, strong capability, or table stakes.
3. For each core competence, list 2–4 other markets or products where it would matter, with the customer job it serves there.
4. Pick the expansion target with the best mix of fit and customer evidence.

## Checks

- [ ] All three tests answered with evidence.
- [ ] Merely good capabilities not promoted.
- [ ] Each expansion names a customer job.
- [ ] Material claims labeled per `references/evidence-rules.md`

## Output

- Competence tests
- Classification
- Expansion options
- Target to explore
- **Signals for the map:** 1–3 candidate opportunities this supports or weakens, each with strength (strong / moderate / weak / assumed) and source IDs

## Reading the signal

A real core competence pointed at a candidate with a clear customer job is a strong advantage signal.

*Method origin: C.K. Prahalad and Gary Hamel.*
