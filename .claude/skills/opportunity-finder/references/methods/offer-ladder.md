# Offer Ladder

**Lens:** Offer · **Method 07 of 21** · **Output:** `opportunity-output/07-offer-ladder.md`

**Question:** How can one valuable outcome be offered at entry, core, premium and recurring levels?

## Why it matters

A single offer forces every customer into one price and commitment. A ladder lowers the first step and creates a path to more value.

## Use when

- Expertise, service or product that could serve customers at different depths or budgets.

## Needs

- Core capability or outcome
- Current offers
- Segment and budget range
- Business profile (`opportunity-output/business-profile.md`)

## Steps

1. Design four rungs: entry (low-risk taste of the outcome), core (main paid delivery), premium (more access, speed or customization), recurring (ongoing value after delivery). State what each includes and excludes.
2. Check each jump is a real step in outcome, access or depth, not repackaging. Flag overlaps.
3. Pick the rung to build first: compare feasibility now with impact on moving customers up; say if they differ.
4. Price ranges are `[HYPOTHESIS]` unless backed by sales data.

## Checks

- [ ] Every rung states exclusions.
- [ ] Jumps are distinct.
- [ ] First rung chosen by feasibility and impact.
- [ ] Material claims labeled per `references/evidence-rules.md`

## Output

- Ladder table
- Progression logic
- Overlaps or gaps
- Rung to build first
- **Signals for the map:** 1–3 candidate opportunities this supports or weakens, each with strength (strong / moderate / weak / assumed) and source IDs

## Reading the signal

A clear entry rung with evidence of demand is a moderate value-capture signal.
