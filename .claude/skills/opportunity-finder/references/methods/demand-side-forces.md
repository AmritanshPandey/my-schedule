# Demand-Side Forces Map

**Lens:** Customer · **Method 04 of 21** · **Output:** `opportunity-output/04-demand-side-forces.md`

**Question:** Will customers actually switch? What pushes and pulls them, and what holds them back?

## Why it matters

Customers agree a new thing is better and still do not switch. Habit and anxiety explain most "great idea, no sales" failures.

## Use when

- Before betting on any new offer or market.
- An idea tests well in conversation but nobody buys.

## Needs

- Segment
- What they use today and why they have not switched
- The proposed alternative
- Business profile (`opportunity-output/business-profile.md`)

## Steps

1. Push: what is wrong with today's situation, specifically.
2. Pull: what is attractive about the new way.
3. Habit: what keeps them where they are (familiarity, sunk cost, integrations, contracts).
4. Anxiety: what worries them about switching (risk, effort, looking foolish, data migration).
5. Rate each force high / medium / low with evidence, and state whether push + pull beat habit + anxiety.
6. Name the one force whose change would shift the balance most, and the specific move (for example, a migration service to reduce anxiety).

## Checks

- [ ] All four forces named with specific reasons.
- [ ] Push and pull kept separate from habit and anxiety.
- [ ] Ratings tied to evidence.
- [ ] Chosen force shifts the balance, not just the most visible.
- [ ] Material claims labeled per `references/evidence-rules.md`

## Output

- Forces table with strength and labels
- Net direction (toward or away from switching)
- Force to act on and the move
- **Signals for the map:** 1–3 candidate opportunities this supports or weakens, each with strength (strong / moderate / weak / assumed) and source IDs

## Reading the signal

Net force toward switching, backed by evidence of actual switching behavior, is a strong "customers act" signal.

*Method origin: Bob Moesta and Chris Spiek (Forces of Progress).*
