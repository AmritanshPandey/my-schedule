# Customer Journey Map

**Lens:** Customer · **Method 03 of 21** · **Output:** `opportunity-output/03-customer-journey-map.md`

**Question:** Where does the customer experience become slow, confusing or costly, and what is that worth?

## Why it matters

Opportunities often sit inside an existing journey: the step where people drop off, call support, or give up.

## Use when

- The problem lives inside an experience the customer already goes through.

## Needs

- Segment
- Journey as observed (touchpoints from trigger to outcome)
- Complaints, drop-off, tickets or churn data
- Business profile (`opportunity-output/business-profile.md`)

## Steps

1. Break the journey into 5–7 stages in the customer's words, from trigger to outcome.
2. For each stage: what they do, think and feel. Mark which parts come from evidence.
3. Locate friction and name its cause, not just the symptom ("insurer portal times out", not "slow").
4. Quantify where possible: drop-off rate, time spent, tickets per stage.
5. Pick the stage to fix first by impact on trust, drop-off or value, not by ease.
6. Say what fixing it would unlock downstream.

## Checks

- [ ] Stages reflect the customer's experience, not internal workflow.
- [ ] Each friction point has a cause.
- [ ] Emotions captured, labeled by evidence.
- [ ] Priority chosen by impact.
- [ ] Material claims labeled per `references/evidence-rules.md`

## Output

- Journey stages table
- Friction points with causes and evidence
- Stage to fix first and what it unlocks
- **Signals for the map:** 1–3 candidate opportunities this supports or weakens, each with strength (strong / moderate / weak / assumed) and source IDs

## Reading the signal

A high-impact friction point with behavioral evidence (drop-off, tickets) is a strong signal for an offer that removes it.

*Method origin: Service design practice.*
