# Three Horizons Map

**Lens:** Strategic Choice · **Method 19 of 21** · **Output:** `opportunity-output/19-three-horizons-map.md`

**Question:** Is effort balanced between defending today, building what is next, and seeding the future?

## Why it matters

Most teams overfund Horizon 1. Opportunities often need a protected Horizon 2 or 3 budget.

## Use when

- Portfolio and planning cycles.

## Needs

- Current initiatives with effort or budget
- Business profile (`opportunity-output/business-profile.md`)

## Steps

1. Sort initiatives: H1 defend and extend the core; H2 build emerging businesses; H3 seed future options. Place by certainty and maturity, not enthusiasm.
2. State the current split of effort or budget, and the target split for this business.
3. Name the most neglected horizon and what to fund next there.
4. Place each candidate opportunity on a horizon.

## Checks

- [ ] Placement by maturity.
- [ ] Current split stated with source.
- [ ] Neglected horizon named.
- [ ] Material claims labeled per `references/evidence-rules.md`

## Output

- Horizons table
- Current vs target split
- What to fund next
- Candidates by horizon
- **Signals for the map:** 1–3 candidate opportunities this supports or weakens, each with strength (strong / moderate / weak / assumed) and source IDs

## Reading the signal

A candidate filling a neglected horizon is a moderate strategic-fit signal.

*Method origin: Mehrdad Baghai, Stephen Coley and David White (McKinsey).*
