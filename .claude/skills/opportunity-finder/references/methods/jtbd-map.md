# Jobs-to-be-Done Map

**Lens:** Customer · **Method 01 of 21** · **Output:** `opportunity-output/01-jtbd-map.md`

**Question:** What progress is the customer trying to make, what triggers the search, and where does today's approach fail?

## Why it matters

People do not buy products; they hire them to make progress in a situation. Naming the job without any product language reveals competitors and openings a feature list hides.

## Use when

- Starting any customer-led or idea-led route.
- The team describes the need in product terms ("they need a dashboard").

## Needs

- Segment
- What customers do, buy or say today (with source IDs)
- Current solutions and workarounds
- Business profile (`opportunity-output/business-profile.md`)

## Steps

1. Write the job as "When I [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [progress]". No product or feature words. If the stated need is a solution, ask what job it stands in for.
2. Find the trigger: the specific moment or event that starts the search for something better.
3. Find the struggle: exactly where the current approach wastes time, money or creates anxiety, and what customers do instead (workarounds are strong evidence).
4. Define "done" in three dimensions: functional (what gets accomplished), emotional (how they want to feel), social (how they want to be seen). Make each measurable where possible.
5. List competing solutions for the job, including non-consumption and doing it by hand.
6. If there is no direct customer evidence, write the job as `[HYPOTHESIS]` and draft 5 interview questions from `assets/templates/interview-guide.md` to test it.

## Checks

- [ ] No product or feature language in the job statement.
- [ ] The trigger is a moment, not a general condition.
- [ ] The struggle names where the current approach breaks.
- [ ] Success has functional, emotional and social criteria.
- [ ] Material claims labeled per `references/evidence-rules.md`

## Output

- Job statement
- Trigger
- Struggle and workarounds
- Success criteria (functional / emotional / social)
- Competing solutions, including non-consumption
- Evidence gaps and interview questions
- **Signals for the map:** 1–3 candidate opportunities this supports or weakens, each with strength (strong / moderate / weak / assumed) and source IDs

## Reading the signal

A job with a frequent trigger, painful workarounds and weak competing solutions is a strong customer signal.
